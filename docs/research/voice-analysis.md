# Voice analysis

## Core voice

Mo’s strongest writing combines first-person candor, an educator’s explanatory instinct, practical examples, and a belief that astrology expands choice rather than replacing it. The voice is warm without being sugary and spiritual without abandoning everyday life.

## Repeating patterns

- Opens with a recognizable lived situation, metaphor, or direct question.
- Moves from emotion or uncertainty into astrological context.
- Explains technique in accessible language, then returns to an actionable reflection.
- Uses contrast: fate/choice, freedom from/freedom to, tenacity/wisdom, space/emptiness.
- Addresses the reader as “you” and includes Mo’s own experience to reduce distance.
- Mixes short emphatic lines with longer, layered explanations.
- Values family, nature, learning, travel, integrity, compassion, and personal sovereignty.

## Recommended editorial principles

1. Lead with the visitor’s situation before naming the technique.
2. Use astrology as a lens for patterns, timing, reflection, and choice.
3. Distinguish observed astronomical timing, established astrological technique, and Mo’s interpretation.
4. Prefer concrete verbs—notice, prepare, compare, ask, reflect, choose—over mystical abstractions.
5. Welcome uncertainty; never turn it into urgency or fear.
6. Preserve technical substance but define terminology at first use.
7. Use first person when the page represents Mo’s approach; use neutral explanatory prose for reference material.

## Sample voice guardrails

Prefer: “A reading can help you notice the patterns and timing around a decision.”

Avoid: “The universe is telling you exactly what to do.”

Prefer: “An eclipse may bring a theme into sharper focus; your response remains your own.”

Avoid: “This eclipse will transform your life.”

## Material needing care

- “Reveal” and “what’s ahead” can imply certainty; use “explore,” “consider,” and “what may be emphasized.”
- “Healing” should not imply medical or mental-health treatment.
- Faith discussion is personal and distinctive, but should remain an invitation rather than a universal claim.
- Quotations attributed to Carl Jung and Alan Oken need source and rights verification before reuse.
